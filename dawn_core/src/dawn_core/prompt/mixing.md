# Mixing doctrine

## Balance first

- Set faders before reaching for processing. A balanced rough mix needs less work.
- Mix into a bus structure: drums, bass, music, vocals or leads, effects. Two or three buses
  are enough for most tracks.
- Keep the master bus clean. Put bus compression on a group, not on the master, unless you
  are mastering.

## EQ

- Cut before you boost. Remove rumble, mud and boxiness first.
- High-pass everything that does not need low end: vocals, guitars, hats, pads, reverbs.
- Use narrow cuts for resonances and broad shelves for tone.
- Do not solo a channel for long. EQ in context.

## Dynamics

- Compress for consistency, not to make something loud. A 2:1 to 4:1 ratio with 3-6 dB of
  gain reduction is a useful starting point.
- Slow attack lets transients through; fast attack controls them. Choose by ear.
- Use parallel compression on drums or vocals when the part needs density without losing
  transients.
- Sidechain the bass to the kick when they fight in the low end. Keep the amount subtle.

## Space

- Use one reverb and one delay as shared returns. Vary the send, not the device.
- High-pass reverb returns. Low-frequency reverb muddies the mix.
- Delay is for rhythm and depth; reverb is for size. Do not use both on everything.
- Check the mix in mono and at low volume before you call it done.

## Common ranges

- Kick: 50-80 Hz fundamental, 2-5 kHz click.
- Bass: 40-120 Hz fundamental, 700 Hz-1.5 kHz definition.
- Snare: 150-250 Hz body, 2-5 kHz crack.
- Vocal: 100 Hz high-pass, 200-400 Hz mud cut, 3-6 kHz presence.
- Hats and cymbals: 6-12 kHz air; high-pass around 300-500 Hz.
