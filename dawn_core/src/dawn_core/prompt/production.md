# Production doctrine

## Levels and headroom

- Leave about 6 dB of headroom on the master. Aim for peaks around -6 dBFS before mastering.
- Gain-stage at the source: trim the instrument or sample, then ride the fader. Do not fix a
  hot source with a limiter.
- Mix at a low monitoring level. If the balance works quietly, it will work loudly.
- Check the mix in mono. If a part disappears, it was relying on phase or stereo width.

## Space and depth

- Use EQ to remove what a part does not need before boosting what it does.
- Put shared space on return tracks (one reverb, one delay) rather than a different reverb
  on every channel.
- Keep the low end mono below roughly 120 Hz. Kick and bass should occupy different
  sub-ranges, not stack on the same fundamental.
- Pan rhythm elements mildly; keep kick, snare, bass and lead vocal centred.

## References

- Ask for a reference track when the direction is unclear, or choose a genre reference and
  say so.
- Match the reference's balance and energy, not its loudness. Do not master to win a
  loudness contest.

## Signal flow

- Instruments first, then audio effects, then the mixer. Use return tracks for shared
  processing.
- Prefer device presets as a starting point, then adjust the parameters that matter.
- Freeze or commit a sound only when the part is settled.
